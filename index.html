The Divide

Free Markets

A portfolio thesis built around capitalism, competition, decentralization, private capital, and entrepreneurship rather than state-directed economic control.

The political vocabulary is shifting: investors, advisors, and voters are increasingly debating free enterprise, socialism, communism, and state-directed economics. FMKT's positioning should be nonpartisan but unmistakable: the Fund is built around the free-market side of that debate.

Free Markets, Not State Control

The economic opposite of socialism

FMKT can be framed around a clear economic contrast: free markets versus state control. On one side are competition, private ownership, entrepreneurship, price signals, and decentralized capital allocation. On the other are socialism, communism, nationalization, central planning, and government-directed allocation of capital.

The Fund does not need to be Republican versus Democrat to own that contrast. The website can speak to the current anti-socialism and anti-communism zeitgeist as an economic framework, while explaining that the portfolio process looks for companies where deregulation and freer-market conditions may matter.

A Disciplined Approach

Turning a free-market thesis into a portfolio

The Fund translates a free-market thesis into an active, multi-faceted investment process. The managers study regulatory friction, potential deregulation, and company fundamentals to identify U.S. businesses they consider aligned with a shift toward freer-market conditions.

Deregulation remains the mechanism: the process targets sectors shaped by government rules, permitting, mandates, capital requirements, and licensing regimes, then scores companies using financial metrics tied to efficiency, profitability, and adaptability.
