The Investment Philosophy

Why free markets matter

Free enterprise rewards the people who build, compete, and take risks. When markets are allowed to allocate capital, innovation, productivity, and opportunity tend to follow. The Free Markets ETF exists for investors who believe that and want a portfolio that reflects it.

The Free Markets ETF (FMKT, NYSE Arca) is an actively managed ETF built around capitalism, free enterprise, economic freedom, and the belief that markets allocate capital more effectively than centralized decision-making. It invests in U.S. companies positioned to benefit from regulatory changes supportive of freer-market dynamics.

What free enterprise makes possible

Every economy answers the same question: who decides how capital is used? Free markets answer it with owners, entrepreneurs, and investors making millions of independent decisions, guided by prices, competition, and the freedom to succeed or fail.

That framework rewards competition, ownership, entrepreneurship, innovation, and opportunity. These are the ideas FMKT is built around. The Fund does not define itself by what it opposes. It defines itself by what it supports.

American enterprise, not partisan politics

There is a distinctly American story behind these ideas: people free to build businesses, entrepreneurs taking risks, investors directing capital, companies competing for customers, and ownership creating accountability. “Pro-America” here means confidence in American enterprise.

FMKT’s thesis is economic. It is not tied to a political party, a candidate, or an election, and it does not predict election outcomes. Its role is to express an economic philosophy through a disciplined fund process.

From philosophy to portfolio

A belief only matters to an investor if it becomes a repeatable process. FMKT connects the two in five steps:

Economic philosophy→Regulatory environment→Company economics→Security selection→Portfolio construction
  1. Economic philosophy. Markets generally allocate capital more effectively than centralized decision-making.
  2. Regulatory environment. Rules, permitting, mandates, capital requirements, and licensing shape where markets are free to operate.
  3. Company economics. The managers assess how changes to that environment could affect a company’s efficiency, profitability, and adaptability.
  4. Security selection. Each candidate is scored on the Fund’s financial metrics and must earn its place.
  5. Portfolio construction. A concentrated portfolio of approximately 30–50 U.S. companies is built through active management.

Deregulation is the investment mechanism

Deregulation is how the Fund’s philosophy becomes an investable idea. It is the mechanism, not the whole identity of the Fund. The portfolio team studies sectors where regulation can meaningfully affect business conditions:

HealthcareFinancial ServicesEnergyTransportationTechnology

The conviction is clear. The construction is disciplined: every position must earn its place through the process.

Frequently asked questions

About FMKT

What is FMKT?

The Free Markets ETF (ticker FMKT, NYSE Arca) is an actively managed ETF built around capitalism, free enterprise, economic freedom, and the belief that markets allocate capital more effectively than centralized decision-making. It invests in U.S. companies the managers expect to benefit from regulatory changes supportive of freer-market dynamics.

What does FMKT invest in?

U.S. companies the managers expect to benefit from shifts in the regulatory environment that are supportive of free-market dynamics, concentrated in sectors shaped by government rules, permitting, mandates, capital requirements, and licensing. Holdings and methodology are described in the Fund’s prospectus.

Is FMKT a political fund?

No. FMKT’s thesis is economic, not partisan. The Fund does not predict elections, advocate for parties or candidates, or tilt toward either major party. It expresses confidence in American enterprise and in markets allocating capital, through a disciplined, actively managed investment process.

The Philosophy

What does it mean to invest in free enterprise?

It means directing capital toward the principles that have long supported American business: freedom to build companies, entrepreneurs taking risk, competition for customers, innovation being rewarded, and ownership creating accountability. FMKT expresses that view through companies positioned to benefit when regulation becomes more supportive of those dynamics.

What is the role of deregulation?

Deregulation is the investment mechanism, not the whole identity of the Fund. The managers evaluate where changes to rules, permitting, mandates, capital requirements, and licensing could affect business conditions, then assess company fundamentals to decide which businesses may be positioned to benefit.

Investing and Process

How does the Fund turn its philosophy into a portfolio?

Through a sequence: economic philosophy, regulatory environment, company economics, security selection, and portfolio construction. The Target, Score, and Build process applies that sequence to build a concentrated portfolio of approximately 30–50 U.S. companies.

How can I invest in FMKT?

FMKT trades on NYSE Arca and can be bought through a standard brokerage account, like any ETF. Investors should consider the investment objectives, risks, charges and expenses carefully before investing and read the prospectus.

Education: Capitalism vs. Socialism

Is there an anti-socialism ETF?

Investors who search for an “anti-socialism ETF” are often looking for a way to express confidence in free markets. FMKT is an actively managed ETF built around that affirmative belief: capitalism, free enterprise, and economic freedom, expressed through U.S. companies positioned to benefit from deregulation and freer-market conditions.

How can I invest against socialism?

FMKT approaches the question by investing for something rather than against something. Investors who believe markets allocate capital better than central planning can buy FMKT through a standard brokerage account. The Fund holds a concentrated, actively managed portfolio of U.S. companies positioned for deregulation and freer-market policy across healthcare, financial services, energy, transportation, and technology.

What is the opposite of socialism?

Socialism and state-directed economics place major decisions about capital and production with the state. Free-market capitalism places them with private owners, entrepreneurs, and investors through voluntary exchange, competition, and price signals. FMKT is built around the second approach as an economic philosophy.

How do free markets compare with state-directed economies?

Economists have long debated how market-based and state-directed systems compare on growth, innovation, and living standards. FMKT’s view is that markets generally allocate capital more effectively than centralized decision-making. This is the Fund’s thesis, not a prediction of results, and it is not investment advice.
